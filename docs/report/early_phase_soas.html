Early Phase SoAs

Early-phase Schedules of Activities, and the representation issues they raise for USDM.

Version: 0.1.0 · 21st September 2026

Summary

Early-phase / experimental-medicine studies — clin-pharm PK and DDI work, PET occupancy, challenge models, and first-in-human dose escalation — produce Schedules of Activities whose cells carry timing structure that a plain activity×timepoint grid cannot hold. This report catalogues those representation issues and asks, for each, whether the CDISC Unified Study Definitions Model (USDM) can carry it.

The question it came from, and still the centre of it: monitoring timelines that span visits and days — a measurement series running as its own schedule across the study-day columns instead of sitting in one cell.

It is a companion to the general SoA and Footnote Patterns report, which catalogued twelve SoA patterns and twenty-one footnote categories across 169 protocols and concluded USDM carries them all. That scan is thin on exactly this population, so this report works the delta.

Status. Against the four tests in docs/aims.md — grounded, mapped, traced, stated — this report carries 6 issues, of which 6 are unmapped against the twelve-pattern atlas and 6 carry no USDM mechanism from it. 6 rest on an unverified transcription rather than on the protocol.

Issues are traced against the SoA pattern atlas and nothing else: the pattern an issue is, and the USDM mechanism the atlas gives for it. The build refuses to print a traced verdict that names neither. Plan: docs/next_steps.md.

Part 1 · What these studies are

A Phase 0 / exploratory study gives a sub-therapeutic exposure under a reduced preclinical package to gather early PK, PD, mechanistic or imaging data — not safety or efficacy, and not therapeutic benefit. The set used here is broader: early-phase experimental medicine.

The operative boundary is not size or duration. It is that the SoA is anchored on an intervention and timed relative to it in sub-day units, rather than on a calendar of visits. A schedule reading "Week 4, Week 8, Week 12" is out however small the study; one reading "predose, 0.5 h, 1 h, 24 h" is in however large.

ClinicalTrials.gov has no "Phase 0" value — these register as Early Phase 1, and much pharma early-phase work is simply labelled Phase 1. Full definition, thresholds and terminology: docs/phase0_definition_and_terms.md.

Part 2 · The protocol set

The set is 36 protocols from ClinicalTrials.gov (real posted protocol documents only), listed with sponsor, indication, SoA form and timing anchor in docs/project_protocols.md. In shape it splits into:

  • Clin-pharm PK / DDI — crossover and drug-drug-interaction designs (Eli Lilly / Loxo), whose SoAs carry the cell-level structure this report is about. The four protocols named in sources/protocols.docx are the primary evidence set, NCT05469126 (J2A-MC-GZGM) above all.
  • PET receptor-occupancy / radioligand-tracer — Asceneuron, Pfizer, Takeda, Genentech, GSK — dose- and scan-anchored, some microdose tracer.
  • Pharmacological- and infection-challenge — LPS, endotoxin, alcohol, amphetamine, influenza, exercise — assessments timed relative to a challenge administration.
  • Imaging microdose — an optical-probe study (NCT01532024).
  • Dose-escalation first-in-human — SAD and MAD — one schedule instantiated per ascending cohort. Added 2026-09-21; the evidence for issue 6, and not yet read.

Sponsor concentration. 15 of the 36 are Eli Lilly (41% of the set), across 17 sponsors in total. A finding drawn from a set this concentrated reads as one sponsor's template until that is fixed or stated — docs/next_steps.md step 5.

Three protocols were removed from the set on 2026-09-21 for falling outside the boundary — NCT02901925 (no SoA at all), NCT03861000 (visits only), NCT04057807 (narrative plus visits). All three remain in protocol_corpus; rejection here is a judgement about this analysis, never about the corpus.

Part 3 · SoA representation issues and USDM impact

Each issue names what the SoA shows, what that demands of the model, where it sits against the twelve-pattern atlas, and the USDM verdict. A verdict marked traced cites the class or rule id it was traced to — the build refuses to print one that does not.

Issue 1 — Interval / duration activities that cross day boundaries

What the SoA shows. A 24-hour urine collection recorded in 8-hour bins (4-hour in some studies), the bins running across the study-day columns and merged to show it:

-24 to -16h, -16 to -8h, -8 to 0h, 0 to 8h, 8 to 16h, 16 to 24h … 48 to 56h, 56 to 64h, 64 to 72h

Unverified. Transcribed from a screenshot in sources/EMP Study.docx, a scoping document, not from the protocol. The 8-hour and 4-hour binning is confirmed in that document's text; the bin list is not. Being re-read from the protocol — docs/next_steps.md step 1.

What it demands. Each bin is an activity with an explicit start and end — a duration, not a point. One logical activity spans several day columns, and the interval can begin before dosing (-24 to -16h).

Atlas: not yet placed. Not yet placed against the atlas.

USDM status: OPEN. USDM timing is expressed primarily as point references — a Timing relative to a reference event, with a window tolerance. Whether a collection interval (start→end as the activity's own extent, distinct from a ± scheduling window) has a first-class representation, or must be modelled as two bounding timings or an activity duration, needs tracing against the atlas. This is the most likely genuine delta. Do not assert it until traced.

Issue 2 — Dose-relative timing in extended-hour notation

What the SoA shows. PK sampling timed in running hours relative to the dose, past 24:

P, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 then 24 (D2), 36 (D2), 48 (D3) … 120 (D26), 168 (D28), 240 (D31) — "Times are relative to LY dosing"

Unverified. The series does not run continuously — 96 (D5) followed by 120 (D26) cannot both be measured from one dose. The likeliest reading is that two crossover periods have been merged, the later hours running from a second dose around Day 21. The pattern — running hours past 24, calendar day in parentheses — is what this issue is about and is not in doubt; the numbers are.

What the SoA shows. The scoping note asks the tool to accept 24:00 hr, 36:00 hr, 48:00 hr … — hours that keep counting rather than resetting to a day-and-clock time. That much is in the document's own text, not a transcription.

What it demands. The primary coordinate is elapsed time from dosing; the "(D2) … (D31)" is the calendar day the offset lands on — a rendering, not the stored value.

Atlas: not yet placed.

USDM status: OPEN. Almost certainly the atlas's pk_profile pattern, which it maps to a dose-anchored sub-timeline. Not yet placed, so not traced. If it is pk_profile, this issue is an existing pattern and not a delta — which would take it out of the report.

Issue 3 — "P": a predose anchor in individual cells

What the SoA shows. A literal P inside cells, per procedure, per period — P for the Day-1 medical assessment and ECG, P, 0.5, 1, 2 … heading a PK series, P (24 hr) for a coproporphyrin draw — re-anchoring in Period 2 of the crossover.

Unverified. The scoping document's text carries the requirement ("a 'P' for predose option for each cell"); the per-cell placements above are transcribed from screenshots.

What it demands. A reusable relative anchor meaning "immediately before the dose in this period", resolving to the correct dose administration in each period.

Atlas: not yet placed.

USDM status: OPEN. Expected to fall inside whichever pattern carries the PK series it heads. Not yet placed. The point to settle: in a multi-period crossover, whether the atlas's treatment of relative anchors covers P re-resolving per period.

Issue 4 — Footnote-encoded scheduling semantics

What the SoA shows. "Heavy usage of footnotes" is the scoping document's own phrase, and is in its text. The cell shows only Xᵇ or Xᶜ; the rule sits in the footnote. Four kinds, all transcribed from screenshots — windows ("within ± 1.5 hours of the scheduled time"); triplicate with borrowed timing ("measured in triplicate at predose and 12h, 24h, 48h, 72h and 96h to match PK collection timing"); conditional repeats ("repeated daily until resolution"); and ordering ("If multiple procedures take place at the same time point, the order should be: ECG, vital signs, blood samples").

Unverified. All four footnote texts are screenshot transcriptions and are being re-read from the protocol.

What it demands. Timing, repetition count, conditionality and ordering — carried as prose attached to a cell, not as the cell mark itself.

Atlas: not yet placed.

USDM status: OPEN. This issue is the atlas's heavy_footnoting pattern by description, and its four parts look like four of the atlas's own footnote categories — window_tolerance, conditional_branch, sample_ordering and something for repeat counts, which may have no category. Not yet placed. If three of the four are existing footnote categories, only the fourth is a delta, and that is the useful result.

Issue 5 — Parallel timelines that start before the dose

What the SoA shows. A sampling series running as its own timeline, beginning the day before dosing and continuing across it — coproporphyrin at 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 hr on Day -1, then P (24 hr) on Day 1 — together with the pre-dose urine bins of issue 1.

Unverified. Transcribed from a screenshot; this series has no textual basis in the scoping document. This is the issue the document was written to raise — monitoring timelines spanning visits and days — so it is the one most worth getting right from the protocol.

What it demands. A second timeline alongside the main dose-anchored one, whose zero is not the dose and which spans the dosing moment.

Atlas: not yet placed.

USDM status: OPEN. Closest atlas candidates are subsidiary_tables and the asynchronous-timeline mechanism. Not yet placed. The angle to settle is whether the atlas covers a sub-timeline whose anchor precedes the main timeline's anchor.

Issue 6 — Dose-escalation cohorts — the same schedule, re-run per cohort

What the SoA shows. A SAD or MAD study prints one SoA and runs it many times: cohort 1 at the lowest dose, cohort 2 at the next, each cohort's schedule identical but for the dose and the calendar it runs on. The table carries no cohort column — that dimension lives in the design narrative and the footnotes. With it come sentinel dosing (a pair dosed first, the rest after a stated interval — a within-cohort split with its own offset), escalation decision gates (cohort n+1 conditional on the safety review of cohort n), and, in MAD, repeat dosing days with a full PK profile on the first and last only and the middle collapsed into one spanning cell.

Unverified. Structural description from the design, not yet from the SoA. Twelve ascending-dose protocols are in the set and none has been read; NCT04586920 carries a reviewer-confirmed count of 6 timelines, the highest, and what drives that is not established.

What it demands. A cohort or dose-level dimension over an otherwise identical schedule, plus a place for the escalation gate to live.

Atlas: not yet placed.

USDM status: OPEN. multi_track is the nearest atlas pattern, at 1.0% the rarest in the scan — which is itself a reason to expect thin coverage here. Whether ascending cohorts are multi_track, or something the atlas has no pattern for, is the question. Not yet placed, evidence not yet read. Nothing about this issue may be stated as a finding.

Where this leaves USDM

The honest position is that no issue in this report is finished. None has been mapped against the twelve-pattern atlas, which is the comparison the whole report is framed on, and none carries a USDM verdict traced to a class or rule id. Three of them (2, 3 and 5) have a reasoned expectation recorded in docs/lessons_learned.md that they fall to mechanisms already understood — but a reasoned expectation is what this project has twice had to retract.

The candidate deltas, on the evidence so far, are issue 1 (an interval with explicit start and end, spanning columns) and parts of issue 4 (repeat counts, conditional repeats, co-timed ordering), with issue 6 unknown until the ascending-dose protocols are read.

The order of work is in docs/next_steps.md: re-quote the evidence from the protocol, then place each issue against the atlas. Placing a mis-transcribed issue is worse than doing nothing.
